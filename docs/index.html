1 What it is

A macOS menu bar app, open source, with no data collection. It answers one question: how much of your Claude plan is left, and are you ahead of or behind the clock. Mac stats are a second tab.

The app is called tokenpanda. The name does not contain "Claude" or "Anthropic", and the popover carries no name at all. Inside the app, "Claude Code", "5h window" and "weekly" are facts and are used as such.

The popover reads like the usage screen of Claude Code opened from the menu bar, drawn on a terminal's dark glass: SF Mono 12, one accent, bars made of block glyphs, aligned mono columns, no cards, no icons in squares.

The assembled popover, Claude tab, with sample data. Hover a figure for its readout.

2 The one idea

Pace, not percent. A used percentage alone says nothing. 61% used is fine on a Saturday and a problem on a Tuesday. So every window bar carries a tick at the elapsed share of the window. Fill behind the tick is time you have already paid for. Fill past the tick is coral: you are spending faster than the clock.

Under the bar, three dim lines say it in words: when the window resets as a clock time, a status word (Ahead, On pace, Over pace) with the projection at the current rate, and what is left per remaining hour. The numbers are computed from four inputs: used percentage, window start, reset time, now. The model is on the spec page with the worked example.

3 The rules it keeps

  • Documented status line feed only. Plan percentages come from the JSON that Claude Code hands its status line script: rate_limits.five_hour and rate_limits.seven_day, each with used_percentage and resets_at. A small hook writes a snapshot file; the app reads it. Nothing else.
  • Zero network. The app makes no network call of its own. No usage endpoint, no price fetch, no update check, no analytics, no crash reports.
  • Nothing stored outside the Mac. The snapshot, the settings and the computed figures stay on the machine. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is synced.
  • No Keychain. The app never reads the Claude Code credential item, never holds a token, never asks the user to sign in.
  • No undocumented endpoint. The app calls no endpoint at all. The consequence is accepted: there is no per model limit bar, because the documented feed does not carry one.
  • List price equivalent, never spent. Token cost is tokens times the published list prices. On a flat plan that is not money spent, and the label says so.
  • Mac figures from public calls. Mach, sysctl, IOKit power sources and libproc. Two keys are best effort because Apple does not document them: GPU utilisation and battery health.
